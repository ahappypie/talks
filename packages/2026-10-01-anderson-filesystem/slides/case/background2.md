# Background Context
* The core Dropbox software product uses block-level synchronization which has been proven reliable at massive scale for many years
* But Dropbox's core competency is infrastructure management: they run their own storage-focused datacenters
* That cost $500M in 2025, up $50M from the year prior
* Dropbox generated nearly $1B of free cash flow in 2025, but carries $2.9B of debt, mostly used to finance hardware leases
* **So what?** Dropbox can fund a focused growth project, but open-ended infrastructure expansion isn't possible: it would compete with debt service, existing lease obligations, and share buybacks, and the current hardware market makes it too difficult to secure additional capacity
